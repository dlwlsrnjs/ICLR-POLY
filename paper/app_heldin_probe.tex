\subsubsection{Frozen probe recipe selected on the held-in panel}
\label{app:heldin-probe-recipe}
The transfer recipe is the modal candidate among the nine held-in leave-one-target-out selections, computed separately for each collection. This is the rule implemented by \texttt{panel\_candidate} in \texttt{heldout\_selector.py}. The selected recipe is \emph{benign reconstruction times borderline detail} for MultiJail and \emph{benign reconstruction times fiction hold} for Lingua-SafetyBench. These are the held-in recipes specified for subsequent closed-model comparisons; this appendix does not report a completed closed-model run using them. A separate historical GPT capstone used a different probe construction and is not evidence that this transfer protocol has already been executed.

\paragraph{What the selected signals measure.}
Both recipes contain reconstruction rates from the existing harmless multilingual probe collection. MultiJail multiplies these by the \texttt{detail} signal measured on 16 safety-adjacent requests designated harmless in the collection. Detail is the mean whitespace-delimited answer length divided by the maximum answer length within the same frame's response batch (with denominator at least one). It is a relative length heuristic, not a human judgment of answer quality. Lingua-SafetyBench instead multiplies reconstruction by \texttt{fiction\_hold}, measured on 16 ordinary harmless requests. For fiction frames this is the fraction of responses matching the stored fiction-structure marker in their first 800 characters; non-fiction frames receive one by definition. Frame signals are shared by configurations with the same answer-language/frame pair. The recorded low-resource answer-language settings are Swahili for MultiJail and Norwegian for Lingua, with per-frame language assignments handled by the collection code. These inputs are existing text probes with collection-specific framing, not a newly substituted generic question list.

\paragraph{Selection evidence and criterion.}
The stored selection experiment evaluates each candidate at three configuration evaluations on nine held-in targets, using 60 replay repetitions per candidate and target, a local RNG seed of zero, and batch-size noise parameters of 64 (MultiJail) or 40 (Lingua). The criterion is mean selector verified ASR on the other eight targets; Spearman correlation is diagnostic and is not the selection objective. Eligible candidates must have measurements for all nine targets and be supported by the deployed candidate loader. The harmful-reconstruction reference, the constant prior, and reduced-coverage diagnostic variants are excluded from recipe selection. Consequently, the target's probe inputs are harmless, but choosing the recipe does use held-in harmful evaluation outcomes; the procedure is not free of harmful supervision during method development.

For a new target, the collection's modal recipe remains fixed while its measured probe values and the selector's online state can change. This distinguishes freezing recipe selection from disabling target-specific adaptation. The experiment supports the recorded empirical choice, not a significance claim for the difference between every pair of candidates.
